### 3. Bike walkaround (6:50, 10 min, runs alongside Stage 1)

The bike is right here, and a few of our open questions can be answered just by looking at it. The bay is too small for everyone at once, so the first 3 or 4 people with WHMIS done who put their hand up go with the project lead, while the new subteam leads start Stage 1 with everyone else. **Look and photograph only. Don't connect, charge or open anything.**

**The battery pack goes first, and only the project lead touches this part.** Before anyone else goes near it, the project lead checks the pack by eye: swelling, leaks or corrosion, torn wrap, bare terminals, warmth or a smell, and whether it's still wired to a controller or harness. Everyone else photographs it where it sits, without lifting, turning or unplugging it. If a label isn't visible, leave it for [#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21). If there's any sign of damage, stop, leave the pack where it is, and tell Ayaan Salim, Electrium's safety captain. Photograph any charger's output label too. Our pack is called 48 V but charges to 50.4 V. A charger whose output says 54.6 V, or that says only "48 V" with no output voltage, is probably for a 13S pack, so tag it "NOT FOR THE 12S PACK".

| Question | What to look for | Post photos on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A motor for Bakfiets is in the bay: which kind? | A thick drum around a wheel's axle means a hub motor. A box at the pedals means a Bafang BBS02. Photograph it, any controller box, and their labels | [#1](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/1) |
| How much of the frame is welded? | Photograph the frame from both sides and note which joints are welded | [#2](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/2) |
| The 2024 battery pack is in the bay: what is it? | Find the pack, and any BMS or charger, and photograph the labels: voltage, capacity, part numbers. Look only | [#7](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/7) |
| Electrium's ESP32 boards and screens: how many, and are they S3? | Count them and read the chip label | **#bakfiets-firm** channel |

What we find here decides a lot. The battery answer settles whether we need to buy or build a pack ([#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21)). The motor answer settles the whole motor controller plan ([#18](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/18)).

## Where things stand (for reference)

| Area | What exists | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical | SolidWorks model, welding jig, notching guides, one FEA study, a partly built frame | Record what's welded (#2), then rerun the FEA with written load cases (#6) |
| Electrical | A power flow diagram, the 2024 battery pack and a motor (both in the bay, untested). The 2024 schematics aren't in any Electrium repo | Confirm the motor (#1) and measure the pack (#21), then draft the power board schematic |
| Firmware | A screen and LED demo with fixed numbers | Show a real battery voltage (#15) and one working button (#14) |

Key numbers people will ask about:

- **Battery:** 12S3P, meaning 12 groups in series with 3 cells in parallel in each. About 44 V normally, 50.4 V full, about 36 V empty. "48 V" is just the class name. See the [pack diagram](../../electrical/diagrams/pack-12s3p.png).
- **Motor:** a motor set aside for Bakfiets is in the bay, and the walkaround tells us which kind. Our working answer is a 500 W hub motor with a Flipsky FSESC 6.7 controller, but the controller only counts as confirmed if a labelled FSESC is found. If none is, it becomes a purchase in #11.
- **Microcontroller:** ESP32-S3, with a 128x64 SSD1306 OLED screen.
- **Anything touching the battery** needs parts rated well above 50.4 V: 100 V MOSFETs and capacitors, and fuses DC-rated for at least 60 V. Surge (TVS) diodes are the one exception, with a standoff of about 54 to 58 V and a clamp voltage below 100 V.
