### 2. Choose the subteam leads (6:40, 10 min)

Each subteam gets one lead. A lead:

- runs their subteam's Discord channel and answers or passes on questions
- keeps their subteam's issues up to date
- approves their subteam's pull requests
- has a short weekly check-in with the project lead about anything blocking the team
- (electrical only) is there for any battery work

Leading isn't about knowing the most. It's about keeping your subteam moving and making sure nobody gets stuck. The project lead backs you up.

**How we'll pick:** anyone interested says so. If one person volunteers for a subteam, they're the lead. If two or more do, they can share it as co-leads, or we take a quick show of hands. If nobody volunteers yet, the project lead covers that subteam for now, and it stays open on [issue #17](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/17).

See the [team structure diagram](../diagrams/team-structure.png) for how the roles fit together.

### 3. Bike walkaround (6:50, 10 min)

The bike is right here, and a few of our open questions can be answered just by looking at it. **Look and photograph only. Don't connect, charge or open anything.**

| Question | What to look for | Post photos on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which motor does it have? | A thick drum around a wheel's axle means a hub motor. A box at the pedals means a Bafang BBS02. Photograph any controller box and its label | [#1](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/1) |
| How much of the frame is welded? | Photograph the frame from both sides and note which joints are welded | [#2](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/2) |
| Is the 2024 battery pack still here? | Find any pack, BMS or charger and photograph the labels: voltage, capacity, part numbers | [#7](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/7) |
| Where are the ESP32 boards and screens? | Check the bay's storage for dev boards and small OLED screens | Firmware channel |

What we find here decides a lot. The battery answer settles whether we need to buy or build a pack ([#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21)). The motor answer settles the whole motor controller plan ([#18](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/18)).

## Where things stand (for reference)

| Area | What exists | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical | SolidWorks model, welding jig, notching guides, one FEA study, a partly built frame | Record what's welded (#2); rerun the FEA with written load cases (#6) |
| Electrical | A power flow diagram. The 2024 schematics were never uploaded | Confirm the motor (#1) and the battery (#7); design the power board from scratch |
| Firmware | A desk demo that shows fixed numbers on the screen | Show a real battery voltage (#15), then read the motor controller (#22) |

Key numbers people will ask about:

- **Battery:** 12S3P, meaning 12 groups in series with 3 cells in parallel in each. About 44 V normally, 50.4 V full, about 36 V empty. "48 V" is just the class name. See the [pack diagram](../../electrical/diagrams/pack-12s3p.png).
- **Motor:** our working answer is a 500 W hub motor with a Flipsky FSESC 6.7 (a VESC). Tonight's walkaround should confirm it.
- **Microcontroller:** ESP32-S3, with a 128x64 SSD1306 OLED screen.
- **Anything touching the battery** needs parts rated well above 50.4 V (80 to 100 V for MOSFETs and capacitors), and fuses DC-rated for at least 60 V.
