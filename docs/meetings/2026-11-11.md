Updates since last week:

- The motor test on 4 Nov worked. The hub motor spun from VESC Tool on the spare bike with the red rear end.
- Our motor controller is a Flipsky Mini FSESC 6.7 Pro on firmware 7.00. Edwin's write-up with the full port map is in [PR #54](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/pull/54).
- The ESP32 will talk to the motor controller over CAN ([#16](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/16)). It needs a CAN transceiver board for that, and picking one is a new task ([#57](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/57)).
- We found two identical brake levers with a built-in motor cut-off in the bay, plus plain levers and 2 m of brake cable and housing. The plan is to use these instead of buying new ones if they work with our calipers, so the Amazon brake order is on hold. The hydraulic options from October are ruled out. The full plan is in [electrical/brake-cutoff.md](../../electrical/brake-cutoff.md).
- We also found a thumb throttle in the bay, and it fits on our handlebar.
- The throttle and the brake levers have Julet 3-pin plugs, so we need short pigtail cables with the matching plug to connect them to our wiring. The purchase request is in for DKARDU Julet 3-pin extension cables (5 pairs, $18.59).
- For torque arms we're requesting the ECSiNG 6 mm stainless pair ($13.80), which fits our 10 mm axle flats.
- The LM2596 buck converter in the bay is rated to 40 V, and our pack reaches 50.4 V, so it can't be our power board. The two options are the Bel DQD20-48-S5-T (20 W) and the DROK 11-90V to 5V 6A converter ($23.99). The total current of the lights decides which one.
- Many of the GitHub issues were rewritten on 8 Nov in plainer language with photos and diagrams. The old text is in a collapsed section at the bottom of each one.

Mechanical (Annie, Aarush):

- Steering ([#25](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/25)). The design follows the 2025 co-op model, and Annie is modelling a stopper so the rod can't hit the cargo box. Look at the ball joints on both ends of the old steering rod. If either one is loose or doesn't turn smoothly, measure its thread size and stud size so we can order new ones.
- Front brake ([#32](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/32)). Hook a lever from the bay up to the Shimano caliper and check it clamps firmly before the lever reaches the grip. Then run a string along the new route for each brake line, with the handlebars turned fully both ways, to check 2 m is enough.
- Rear brake ([#34](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/34)). Take the caliper and rotor off the green mountain bike. Photograph them, check if the caliper is post mount or IS mount, and measure the rotor.
- Torque arms ([#31](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/31)). Each arm screws into an M5 threaded hole on the frame. Check if our dropouts have one within reach of the arm's slot. If they don't, we'll use hose clamps for now.
- Rear stays ([#37](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/37)). Since the stays get rewelded anyway, a small threaded tab for each torque arm could go on at the same time.
