### 2. The motor, battery and budget (6:40, 15 min)

**Motor: the 2000 W hub motor.** It's already in a wheel and drives it directly. The Flipsky 6374 would need about a 30:1 gear reduction (Jack's numbers on [#1](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/1)). Ontario limits e-bikes to 500 W and 32 km/h, so the motor controller gets set to 500 W in VESC Tool. That's part of the bench test ([#18](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/18)).

**Battery: Pack 4, the 48 V (12S2P) pack.** It matches the voltage the rest of the bike is designed for (50.4 V full). It has fewer cells than the 12S3P we first planned, so expect less range: if its cells are about 3 Ah, roughly 6 Ah instead of 9 Ah. The capacity test in #21 gives the real number. Nobody uses it until it passes the checks in [#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21) with the electrical lead there. Until then, look only: don't charge, plug in or open any pack.

**Budget: about $300** (from Samantha). We already have most of what we need, so it goes to two things. First, the safety parts that Pack 4 and the motor need before anything gets powered: a 12S charger and BMS if Pack 4 doesn't have them, a DC fuse, an antispark plug and torque arms. Expect about $150 for those. Second, the steering, which is the top mechanical priority and gets the rest. The bike also has no brakes yet, so brakes need a share too. Every purchase is posted on its issue with a link and price, and only the project lead orders, through Electrium. Don't buy parts yourself.

**The 500 W rule.** Ontario's e-bike rule goes by the motor's rated power, 500 W or less, not by a software limit. Our motor is labelled 2000 W, so even set to 500 W it may not count as an e-bike. Until that's settled, the bike is only ridden off public roads.
