### 2. The motor, battery, budget and parts order (6:40, 15 min)

**Motor: the 2000 W hub motor.** It's already in a wheel and drives it directly. The Flipsky 6374 would need about a 30:1 gear reduction (Jack's numbers on [#1](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/1)). We'll set the controller to 500 W and 32 km/h in VESC Tool as part of the bench test ([#18](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/18)).

**Battery: Pack 4, the 48 V (12S2P) pack.** It matches the voltage the rest of the bike is designed for (50.4 V full). It has fewer cells than the 12S3P we first planned, so expect less range: if its cells are about 3 Ah, roughly 6 Ah instead of 9 Ah. The capacity test in #21 gives the real number. Nobody uses it until it passes the checks in [#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21) with the electrical lead there. Until then, look only: don't charge, plug in or open any pack.

**Budget: about $300** (from Samantha). Since we already have the BMS, charger and antispark, the only safety parts left are a DC fuse and torque arms, roughly $40 to $80. That leaves about $220 to $260 for steering and brakes. Post anything you need on its issue with a link and price, and the project lead orders it through Electrium. Don't buy parts yourself.

**Parts order before reading week.** To give Samantha and the co-op students time to process it, anything we need to buy should be posted on its issue by **Fri 9 Oct**. The project lead puts the order in that weekend. Steering is a second order after reading week, once a design is picked.

**The 500 W rule.** Ontario's e-bike regulation ([O. Reg. 369/09](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/090369)) uses the federal definition of a power-assisted bicycle, which caps the motor's continuous power rating at 500 W. Ours is rated 2000 W, so a software limit probably isn't enough. The project lead has asked Ling what this means for a club prototype. Until then, no riding on public roads.
