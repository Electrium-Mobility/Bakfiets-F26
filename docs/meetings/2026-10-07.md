### 2. Decide: motor and battery (6:40, 15 min)

**Motor.** The project lead's recommendation is the 2000 W hub motor.

| | 2000 W hub motor | Flipsky 6374 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drives the wheel | Directly, it's already in a wheel | Needs a gear reduction of about 30:1 (Jack's numbers on #1) |
| Work for mechanical | Check it fits the frame's dropouts | Design and build a reduction stage |
| Made for | E-bikes, 48 V | Electric skateboards |

Whichever we pick, Ontario limits e-bikes to 500 W and 32 km/h, so the motor controller has to cap the power. That goes into the bench test plan ([#18](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/18)).

**Battery.** Pack 4 (48 V, 12S2P) matches the voltage the rest of the bike is designed for (50.4 V full). It has fewer cells than the 12S3P we planned, so expect less range: roughly 6 Ah instead of 9 Ah. The 36 V pack is too low for a 48 V motor. Before anyone uses Pack 4, we need to:

- confirm with Samantha who the packs belong to and whether we can use one
- run the checks in [#21](https://github.com/Electrium-Mobility/Bakfiets-F26/issues/21) with the electrical lead there. Look only until then: don't charge, plug in or open any pack
